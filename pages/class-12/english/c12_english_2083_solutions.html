Section A: Comprehension

15 Marks

The Magical Library

In a quiet corner of the bustling city, there stood an old, dusty library. While most people hurried past, its unassuming exterior hid an incredible secret. The librarian, an elderly woman named Ms. Abernathy, was not an ordinary librarian; she was the guardian of a magical library.

One sunny afternoon, Sarah, an eighth-grader with an insatiable love for books, stumbled upon the library. She had always been drawn to stories of magic and adventure, and something about the library's unassuming facade piqued her curiosity. As she stepped inside, a small bell above the door tinkled, announcing her arrival. Ms. Abernathy greeted her with a warm smile, sensing Sarah’s keen interest in books.

Sarah soon discovered the true enchantment of the library. Its shelves held volumes that could transport readers to other worlds. She found herself immersed in tales of knights and dragons, enchanted forests, and talking animals. These books had a way of making her feel like she was right there, part of the adventure.

As the weeks passed, Sarah spent hours at the library, delving into one captivating story after another. She uncovered a book about a boy who could fly, and as she read, she felt her feet lifting off the ground. She found herself soaring over the city, experiencing the thrill of flight. Another day, she read about a time-traveling watch, and suddenly, she was witnessing historical events unfold before her eyes.

One day, as Sarah was browsing the shelves, she noticed a book titled “The Lost Amulet of Althoria.” The cover featured a golden amulet with a shimmering blue gem. Ms. Abernathy explained that this was a unique book—it was unfinished. No one knew how the story ended because no reader had ever returned the book. Sarah decided to take on the challenge. She opened the book and was immediately drawn into a tale of a young adventurer, Alaric, on a quest to find the lost amulet. She felt herself journeying through dark forests, crossing treacherous rivers, and facing mythical creatures. The more she read, the deeper she became entangled in the story. It was as though she had become Alaric, feeling his determination and bravery.

As the days turned into weeks, Sarah's friends and family grew concerned. She had become so absorbed in the book that she neglected her usual activities. Ms. Abernathy gently reminded her to take breaks, but Sarah was determined to reach the end of the story.

Finally, one evening, as Sarah read the last page, she found herself standing in a clearing, facing an ornate door. Through the door, she saw a dazzling chamber with the lost amulet resting on a pedestal. With a deep breath, she opened the door and approached the amulet. As she touched it, a burst of blue light enveloped her, and she felt herself returning to the library.

When she opened her eyes, she was back among the dusty bookshelves, holding the book in her hands. Ms. Abernathy congratulated her on completing the unfinished story. Sarah had not only finished the tale but had also experienced an adventure unlike any other.

From that day on, Sarah continued to visit the magical library, understanding that the real magic was not just in the stories but in the way they made her feel and the adventures they allowed her to live.

1(C)
Answer the following questions in short. [5 × 1 = 5 Marks]
  1. Why did Sarah go to the library in the beginning?
  2. What happened when Sarah reads the book about a boy who can fly?
  3. What was special about the book “The Lost Amulet of Althoria”?
  4. What was Alaric searching for in the tale?
  5. Was Sarah more determined to read books at the end? Give a reason.
check_circle Short Answer Solutions
  1. Why did Sarah go to the library in the beginning?
    Sarah went to the library initially because she had an insatiable love for books, was naturally drawn to stories of magic and adventure, and the library's unassuming facade piqued her curiosity.
  2. What happened when Sarah reads the book about a boy who can fly?
    When Sarah read the book about a boy who could fly, she felt her feet lifting off the ground and experienced the sensation of soaring over the city.
  3. What was special about the book “The Lost Amulet of Althoria”?
    The book was unique and unfinished because no previous reader had ever returned it, meaning no one had ever reached or known its ending.
  4. What was Alaric searching for in the tale?
    Alaric was searching for the lost amulet of Althoria, an ornate golden amulet adorned with a shimmering blue gemstone.
  5. Was Sarah more determined to read books at the end? Give a reason.
    Yes, Sarah became even more determined to read books because she realized that true magic lived not just in the words, but in how stories made her feel and the extraordinary adventures they allowed her to live.

Section B: Literature

20 Marks
Question 2: Short Answer Questions [5 × 2 = 10 Marks]
2(a)
How would you react if you were in the young man's situation at the end of the story? (Neighbours) [2 Marks]
check_circle Model Answer

If I were in the young husband's situation at the end of Tim Winton's “Neighbours”, I would feel deeply moved, humbled, and grateful. Witnessing the overwhelming, genuine joy and collective celebration of the European immigrant neighbours upon the birth of his newborn child would instantly dissolve all my previous prejudices and cultural wariness. Like the young husband, I would weep tears of gratitude, realizing that human empathy, love, and neighbourly solidarity transcend all linguistic and cultural boundaries.

2(b)
How was Gouvernail different from Mrs. Baroda's expectation? (A Respectable Woman)
OR Do you think Rakesh was a devoted son? Explain. (A Devoted Son) [2 Marks]
check_circle Model Answers (Both Options Covered)

Option A (A Respectable Woman):
Mrs. Baroda had expected Gouvernail to be a demanding, cynical, and overly intellectual city man who would disrupt their quiet, peaceful plantation life. Contrary to her expectations, she found him to be remarkably gentle, quiet, lovable, and unassuming. He made no effort to impress anyone, spent hours quietly enjoying the country air, and spoke with an unpretentious warmth that surprisingly charmed her.

Option B (A Devoted Son):
Initially, Rakesh was an exemplary devoted son who honoured his parents by touching his father's feet and attributing all his medical accolades to them. However, when his father Varma fell ill in old age, Rakesh's devotion shifted into rigid, clinical, and authoritarian control. He strictly restricted his father's favourite foods and ignored his emotional dignity. Thus, while professionally dutiful, his devotion became emotionally suffocating rather than genuinely compassionate.

2(c)
What does the poet say about God's greatness? (Every Morning I Wake) [2 Marks]
check_circle Model Answer

In “Every Morning I Wake”, Dylan Thomas portrays God's greatness through His boundless mercy, benevolence, and unconditional love for ordinary, mortal human beings. Rather than depicting an intimidating and wrathful deity, the poet presents God as a caring guardian who watches over the frail, imperfect, and vulnerable residents of Milk Wood, protecting them through the dark of night and blessing their simple everyday lives.

2(d)
Where was the author when he was late for lunch? (On Libraries)
OR What is the goal of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights? (Human Rights and the Age of Inequality) [2 Marks]
check_circle Model Answers (Both Options Covered)

Option A (On Libraries):
In Oliver Sacks' essay “On Libraries”, the author was deeply immersed in the world of books inside a library (specifically the British Museum Library / Willesden Green Library). Completely absorbed in researching and reading scientific volumes, he lost all sense of time and physical surroundings, which caused him to be late for his lunch appointment.

Option B (Human Rights and the Age of Inequality):
The primary goal of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR, 1948) is to establish a universal baseline of inherent human dignity, protecting all individuals from state cruelty, tyranny, discrimination, and political persecution. Samuel Moyn notes that while it established vital political and civil liberties, its framework has struggled to address the massive economic disparities produced by modern neoliberalism.

2(e)
Do you think that August Strindberg portrays despair in a very powerful and realistic way? Give a reason. (Facing Death) [2 Marks]
check_circle Model Answer

Yes, August Strindberg portrays despair with stark realism and tragic intensity through Monsieur Durand in “Facing Death”. Durand's despair is deeply realistic because it arises from tangible realities—crushing financial bankruptcy, starvation, and the bitter hatred of his own daughters whom he silently sacrificed everything for. His ultimate resolution to set fire to his own boarding house and perish inside to secure fire insurance money reflects the tragic, desperate extremes a hopeless person will reach.

Question 3: Long Answer Questions [2 × 5 = 10 Marks]
3(a)
What was the reaction of the community to the old man with enormous wings and what does it show about humanity and compassion? (A Very Old Man with Enormous Wings)
OR What are Nock's ideas about marriage in society? Do you agree or disagree with him? Why? (Marriage as a Social Institution) [5 Marks]
check_circle Model Answers (Detailed Analysis)

Option A (A Very Old Man with Enormous Wings):
In Gabriel García Márquez's story, the community reacts to the arrival of the winged old man with superficial fascination, monetary greed, and profound cruelty. Instead of viewing him as an angel or displaying genuine charity, Pelayo and Elisenda cage him in their filthy chicken coop and charge five cents per admission. The villagers treat him like a circus oddity—poking him with hot branding irons, throwing stones, and plucking his feathers when he fails to perform miracles. Father Gonzaga doubts his sanctity simply because he cannot speak Latin. Eventually, when a spider-woman arrives, the fickle public completely abandons him.

This reaction exposes the dark side of human nature: people often dehumanize, commercialize, and abuse what they cannot comprehend. True spiritual compassion is supplanted by selfish greed and amusement. It demonstrates that human sympathy is often shallow and conditional, quickly turning hostile when others fail to meet conventional expectations.

Option B (Marriage as a Social Institution):
In “Marriage as a Social Institution,” Stephen L. Nock argues that marriage is fundamentally a public social institution rather than merely an intimate, private romance. As an institution, marriage is governed by social norms, legal obligations, and cultural expectations that define marital roles, encourage fidelity, and establish shared responsibility for raising children and sustaining economic stability.

I agree with Nock's perspective. While romantic love is necessary for mutual affection and emotional bonding, romantic feelings fluctuate over time. The institutional framework of marriage provides enduring structure, legal rights, and social accountability that keep families stable through life's trials. Viewing marriage as a social institution protects the interests of children and creates a cohesive, resilient society.

3(b)
Explain the following lines of the poem, 'A Day' with reference to the context: [5 Marks]
The hills untied their bonnets,
The bobolinks begun.
Then I said softly to myself,
That must have been the sun!
check_circle Context & Detailed Explanation

Context: These lines are extracted from the renowned nature poem “A Day” composed by American poet Emily Dickinson. In this poem, the speaker presents a child-like, awe-inspiring observation of nature as it transitions through sunrise and sunset.

Explanation: In these specific lines, the speaker captures the majestic arrival of dawn using vivid personification and auditory imagery:

  • “The hills untied their bonnets” is a charming personification comparing the morning mist lifting from the hilltops to women untying and removing their hats (bonnets) to greet the day. As the sunlight touches the earth, shadows disappear and the green landscape is revealed.
  • “The bobolinks begun” introduces auditory imagery, describing the sudden, melodic morning singing of the bobolinks (songbirds) as they awaken to the sunlight.
  • “Then I said softly to myself, / That must have been the sun!” highlights the speaker's gentle revelation and reverence. Having observed the unveiling of the hills and the burst of birdsong, the speaker humbly concludes that the glorious force behind this magical awakening is none other than the sun.

These lines celebrate the effortless majesty and transformative energy with which morning light brings the world to life.

Study the following table and interpret the information given in the table in about 150 words. Make comparisons where necessary. [7 Marks]
Monthly Sales Data for XYZ Company (in thousands of dollars)
Month Product A Product B Product C Total Sales
January 50 40 30 120
February 60 35 25 120
March 70 45 35 150
April 65 50 40 155
May 75 55 45 175
June 80 60 50 190
check_circle Model Interpretation

The provided table illustrates the monthly sales figures (in thousands of dollars) for three products—Product A, Product B, and Product C—manufactured by XYZ Company over a six-month period from January to June. Overall, the company witnessed a strong upward growth trajectory, with total monthly sales increasing significantly from $120,000 in January and February to a peak of $190,000 in June.

Among the three items, Product A was the primary revenue driver throughout the entire semester. Its sales commenced at $50,000 in January, experienced steady gains to reach $70,000 in March, recorded a minor dip to $65,000 in April, and ultimately reached an all-time high of $80,000 in June. Product B ranked second overall; after a slight drop from $40,000 in January to $35,000 in February, it achieved consistent month-on-month growth, culminating at $60,000 in June. Product C recorded the lowest revenue, beginning at $30,000, touching a low of $25,000 in February, and steadily expanding to $50,000 by June.

In conclusion, all three product lines registered their maximum performance in June, highlighting a particularly strong second quarter for XYZ Company.

It is often said, 'Save nature to save the future'. Write a letter to the editor of a national newspaper about the effects of environmental pollution in about 180 words. [8 Marks]
check_circle Model Formal Letter

Kathmandu, Nepal
May 23, 2026

To,
The Editor,
The Rising Nepal,
Kathmandu, Nepal.

Subject: The Alarming Effects of Environmental Pollution.

Sir,

Through the esteemed columns of your widely circulated daily, I wish to voice serious concern regarding the escalating environmental degradation across Nepal. The proverb, “Save nature to save the future,” serves as a critical warning that requires immediate collective action.

Rapid and unplanned urbanization, unchecked industrial discharges, and vehicular emissions have severely deteriorated our air and water quality. Kathmandu regularly ranks among the world's most polluted cities during winter, exposing citizens—particularly children and senior citizens—to debilitating respiratory and cardiovascular illnesses. Furthermore, our sacred rivers, including the Bagmati, have been converted into open conduits for untreated sewage and plastic waste. In the hills, rampant deforestation intensifies seasonal landslides, soil erosion, and erratic weather patterns.

If we do not preserve our ecosystems today, we jeopardize the well-being and survival of future generations. The government must strictly enforce emission standards, transition toward electric public transit, and introduce community waste-segregation policies. Individual citizens must also actively adopt eco-friendly lifestyles.

Yours faithfully,
A Concerned Citizen

Write an essay on walking as a physical activity and its role in building physical, mental and social behavior of an individual in about 300 words. [10 Marks]
check_circle Model Essay

Walking: A Holistic Path to Well-being

Walking is one of the most natural, fundamental, and universally accessible physical activities available to humanity. Requiring no costly memberships, elaborate gear, or specialised coaching, it provides an invaluable pathway to nurturing an individual's physical vitality, psychological clarity, and social engagement.

From a physical perspective, regular brisk walking serves as a cornerstone of preventive health. Engaging in a thirty-minute walk daily strengthens the cardiovascular system, improves blood circulation, and optimizes arterial pressure. Because it is a low-impact exercise, it gently conditions muscle groups and lubricates joint cartilage without causing musculoskeletal strain. Over time, it helps regulate body weight, boosts basal metabolism, and significantly reduces the incidence of chronic lifestyle ailments such as type 2 diabetes, stroke, and cardiac disorders.

Mentally, walking serves as a potent therapeutic intervention. Physical locomotion stimulates the production of neurochemicals like endorphins and serotonin, which naturally elevate mood and combat psychological exhaustion. Spending time walking in open green spaces—commonly referred to as “green exercise”—quiets mental rumination, relieves anxiety, and sharpens cognitive focus. It provides a sanctuary from digital screen fatigue, offering individuals valuable solitude to reflect, untangle complex thoughts, and spark creativity.

Socially, walking acts as an essential catalyst for community connection. Walking through neighborhood trails, public parks, or pedestrian plazas encourages spontaneous greetings and meaningful conversations, curbing modern feelings of urban loneliness and social isolation. Walking clubs and communal walking groups foster teamwork, mutual accountability, and intergenerational empathy. Furthermore, opting to walk rather than drive short commutes diminishes vehicular emissions, modeling environmentally conscientious civic behavior.

In conclusion, walking is far more than simple locomotion; it is a holistic lifestyle habit that revitalizes the body, pacifies the mind, and knits society closer together. Cultivating a daily walking routine is an empowering investment in a healthier, calmer, and more connected future.

Section D: Grammar

10 Marks
7
Do as instructed in the brackets and rewrite the sentences. [10 × 1 = 10 Marks]
check_circle Grammar Solutions & Structural Explanations
a. Health care in Nepal is not as expensive as it is in the US. (Identify the adjective in the sentence.)
→ Answer: expensive
info Context: 'Expensive' is a qualitative adjective appearing in the equative degree structure 'as + adjective + as'.
b. My car needs ............ (repair/repairing/to repair/repaired) right now. (Choose the correct form of the verb.)
→ Answer: repairing
info Rule: 'Need + Gerund (V-ing)' conveys passive necessity (i.e. 'needs to be repaired').
c. This is the house. We spent our childhood in the house. (Join using appropriate relative pronoun.)
→ Answer: This is the house where we spent our childhood.
info Alternative: This is the house in which we spent our childhood. 'Where' acts as a relative adverb of place.
d. Sumsher along with his friends ........... (know/knows/have known/knowing) me well. (Subject-verb agreement.)
→ Answer: knows
info Rule: In phrases joined by 'along with', 'with', or 'as well as', the verb agrees with the primary subject ('Sumsher' = singular).
e. Don't stare ......... me. I have no mistake. (Use appropriate preposition.)
→ Answer: at
info Rule: 'Stare at', 'glance at', and 'look at' take the preposition 'at' when indicating direction of gaze.
f. You ....... (may/must/would/could) not tell anyone about this. It's too confidential. (Choose modal verb.)
→ Answer: must
info Rule: 'Must not' expresses strong prohibition required by high confidentiality.
g. The customer ............ the machine before he complained about the error. (Correct form of 'purchase'.)
→ Answer: had purchased
info Rule: Past perfect ('had purchased') describes the earlier action completed before another past event ('complained').
h. We are close friends. We have not seen each other's houses. (Join with 'despite'.)
→ Answer: Despite being close friends, we have not seen each other's houses.
info Alternative: Despite the fact that we are close friends, we have not seen each other's houses.
i. Yangma said, “You are my source of inspiration in life.” (Change into indirect speech.)
→ Answer: Yangma told me that I was his/her source of inspiration in life.
info Rule: Present tense 'are' changes to past 'was'; 1st person possessive 'my' shifts to 'his/her'.
j. Loku was presenting his project for the first time. (Change into passive voice.)
→ Answer: His project was being presented by Loku for the first time.
info Rule: Past continuous passive uses 'was/were + being + past participle (V3)'.

Section E: Vocabulary & Sounds

5 Marks
8
Choose and copy the correct alternatives. [5 × 1 = 5 Marks]
check_circle Solutions with Linguistic Explanations
a. It's a beautiful present for me. What is the word class of the underlined word 'present'?
(i) noun  •  (ii) adjective  •  (iii) adverb  •  (iv) verb
→ Answer: (i) noun
info Explanation: 'Present' here is preceded by an article ('a') and an adjective ('beautiful'), functioning as the direct object noun meaning 'gift'.
b. Which of the following words comes before the word 'choice' in the dictionary?
(i) choose  •  (ii) chrome  •  (iii) chide  •  (iv) chord
→ Answer: (iii) chide
info Alphabetical check: 'ch-i' precedes 'ch-o'. Hence 'chide' comes before 'choice', whereas choose, chord, and chrome appear after.
c. The babysitter ........... the girl while her parents were not at home.
(i) looked after  •  (ii) looked at  •  (iii) looked over  •  (iv) looked up
→ Answer: (i) looked after
info Phrasal Verb: 'Look after' means to take care of or tend to someone.
d. Which of the following words takes the prefix 'il-'?
(i) balance  •  (ii) fertile  •  (iii) legal  •  (iv) mobile
→ Answer: (iii) legal
info Prefix Rule: Root words beginning with 'l' assimilate the negative prefix 'in-' into 'il-' → 'illegal' (vs imbalance, infertile, immobile).
e. Which of the following words has the same initial sound as in the word 'chaos'?
(i) chance  •  (ii) chemical  •  (iii) circus  •  (iv) cinema
→ Answer: (ii) chemical
info Phonetics: 'Chaos' begins with the voiceless velar plosive /k/ (/ˈkeɪ.ɒs/), matching 'chemical' (/ˈkem.ɪ.kəl/). In contrast, 'chance' starts with /tʃ/, and 'circus'/'cinema' with /s/.
