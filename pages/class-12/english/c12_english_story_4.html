The Treasure in the Forest

H.G. Wells • Short Story 4

Summary of “The Treasure in the Forest”

“The Treasure in the Forest” by H. G. Wells is a mysterious adventure story about two men, Evans and Hooker, who travel by canoe to a remote tropical island in search of hidden treasure. They have learned about the treasure from three Chinese men and have obtained a map showing its location. The treasure had originally been hidden by Chang-hi, who had discovered gold ingots left behind from a Spanish shipwreck.

After reaching the island, Evans and Hooker follow the directions on the old map and enter the dense forest. They eventually find a dead Chinese man near a freshly dug hole. Inside the hole, they discover several gold ingots. Despite the disturbing discovery, their desire for wealth makes them focus on taking the gold with them.

They place the gold on Evans’s coat and try to carry it towards their canoe. However, Evans soon becomes weak and experiences severe pain. While handling the gold, he had been pricked by a tiny thorn. Later, Hooker is also pricked by a thorn while picking up the ingots.

Hooker then realizes the terrible truth: the thorns surrounding the treasure have been poisoned. He understands that the dead Chinese man had also been killed by the poison and that Chang-hi had deliberately protected the treasure with these deadly thorns. Evans eventually becomes motionless, while Hooker also begins to suffer from the poison.

The story ends with Hooker sitting helplessly beside the gold while the poison spreads through his body. The story shows how greed for wealth can make people ignore danger and ultimately lead to their destruction.

Key Moral: Greed can lead people to ignore danger and ultimately bring about their own destruction. Excessive desire for wealth can make people reckless and blind to risk.

Understanding the Text

Answer the following questions:

a. Describe the expository scene of the story.

The story begins with Evans and Hooker travelling in a canoe towards an island in search of hidden treasure. They approach the mouth of a small river that flows into the sea. The area is surrounded by thick green forest, while mountains can be seen in the distance. The sea is calm, but the heat of the sun is intense.

b. What does the map look like and how do Evans and Hooker interpret it?

The map is an old, rough and badly worn piece of paper. It has been folded so many times that it is creased and almost torn apart. It shows the outline of the bay, the river, a reef, a group of palm trees and a star marking the location of the treasure. Evans identifies the twisting line as the river, while Hooker explains how the straight and dotted lines can be used to locate the treasure.

c. How did Evans and Hooker know about the treasure?

Evans and Hooker learned about the treasure by secretly listening to three Chinese men talking. They discovered that Chang-hi had found gold ingots that had been hidden for about two hundred years after a Spanish shipwreck. Chang-hi had also made a map showing the location of the treasure.

d. Describe Evans’s dream.

While travelling in the canoe, Evans became tired and fell into a light sleep. In his dream, he remembered the night when he and Hooker had discovered the Chinese men’s secret. He imagined the three Chinese men sitting near a fire and discussing the buried treasure. The dream then became disturbing: he saw Chang-hi struggling to prevent him from taking the gold. The gold changed into a burning furnace, and a strange devil resembling Chang-hi appeared. Evans was eventually awakened when someone called his name.

e. What do the two treasure hunters see when they walk towards the island?

As they move through the forest, Evans and Hooker see thick vegetation, large trees, flowers and creepers. After reaching the river, they discover a clearing where they find a dead Chinese man lying on the ground. Near him, they also see a Chinese-style spade, a freshly dug hole and a pile of stones.

f. In what condition did the treasure hunters find the dead man?

They found the dead Chinese man lying face down in a clear area of the forest. His neck was swollen and purple, and his hands and ankles were also swollen. His body was near a freshly dug hole where the treasure had been buried.

g. How did the treasure hunters try to carry the gold ingots to the canoe?

They removed Evans’s coat and placed several gold ingots on it. Then they held the corners of the coat and lifted the gold together, intending to carry it towards the canoe. However, the weight of the gold soon made Evans extremely tired.

h. How were Evans and Hooker poisoned?

Both men were poisoned by tiny poisonous thorns hidden around the treasure. Evans was pricked by one of them while handling the gold. Later, Hooker was also pricked on his thumb by a slender thorn. After seeing the thorn, Hooker realized that the thorns had been deliberately poisoned, like those used by the Dyaks in their blowing-tubes. The poison gradually affected both men, leaving Evans helpless and causing Hooker to develop pain and difficulty moving his fingers.

Reference to the Context

a. How do you know the story is set on a tropical island?

The story “The Treasure in the Forest” is set on a tropical island. The story describes a hot and humid environment with thick green forests, palm trees, a coral reef, a lagoon and a small river flowing into the sea. The intense heat of the sun, tropical vegetation, colourful flowers and distant mountains also support the idea that the setting is a tropical island.

b. Why do you think Evans and Hooker took such a risk to find the buried treasure on the island?

Evans and Hooker took such a great risk mainly because of their greed and desire for wealth. They had learned about a large amount of gold hidden on the island and were determined to find it despite the difficult journey and dangerous surroundings. The possibility of gaining great wealth made them ignore the risks involved. Their behaviour shows how greed can make people careless about their own safety and the possible consequences of their actions.

c. Do you think the narrator of the story is racist? If yes, what made him feel superior to other races?

The story contains racially prejudiced attitudes, especially in Evans’s thoughts and in the language used to describe the Chinese characters. For example, Evans thinks that the life of a Chinese person is less sacred than that of a European. He also later says, “All Chinamen are alike.” These statements show the racial prejudice held by the characters.
However, it is not accurate to say that the narrator himself is definitely racist based on these examples. The narrator is reporting the thoughts and words of Evans and Hooker rather than necessarily expressing those views personally. The story does, however, portray the racial attitudes of its characters, which were significant to the context of the story.

d. What do you think is the moral of the story?

The main moral of the story is that greed can lead people to ignore danger and ultimately bring about their own destruction. Evans and Hooker become so focused on obtaining the gold that they fail to consider the warning signs around them. Even after finding the dead Chinese man, they continue to handle and carry the treasure. Eventually, they are poisoned by the hidden thorns protecting the gold.
The story therefore shows that excessive desire for wealth can make people reckless and blind to danger. It teaches us that we should not allow greed to control our decisions, because the desire for material wealth can have serious consequences.

Reference Beyond the Text

a. Interpret the story as a mystery story.

“The Treasure in the Forest” can be interpreted as a mystery story because it creates suspense and keeps the readers curious about what will happen next. From the beginning, Evans and Hooker travel to a remote island with an old map to search for hidden treasure. The map itself is mysterious, as some of its marks are difficult for them to understand.

The mystery becomes deeper when they find a dead Chinese man near a freshly dug hole and discover that the treasure is still there. Although they find the gold, strange thorns around the treasure make the situation dangerous. At first, they do not understand the purpose of the thorns. Later, when Hooker is pricked by one, he realizes that they have been poisoned and understands the warning hidden in the map.

The isolated forest, unexplained death, mysterious map, hidden treasure and poisonous thorns gradually reveal the secret behind the treasure. These unexpected events and the suspenseful ending make the story a mystery as well as an adventure story.

b. Treasure hunting is a favorable subject of children’s stories. Remember a treasure-hunting story you read in your childhood and compare and contrast it with The Treasure in the Forest.

I have read Treasure Island by Robert Louis Stevenson, which is also a famous treasure-hunting story. Both Treasure Island and The Treasure in the Forest revolve around the search for hidden treasure and involve journeys to remote islands. In both stories, a map or information about the location of treasure motivates the characters to undertake a dangerous journey.

However, the two stories are different in their characters, atmosphere and plot. Treasure Island mainly follows Jim Hawkins, a young boy who becomes involved in a search for pirate treasure. It is an adventure story involving pirates, a sea voyage, conflicts and struggles among the characters. In contrast, The Treasure in the Forest focuses on Evans and Hooker, two men whose desire for gold leads them into increasing danger.

The atmosphere is also different. Treasure Island has a broader adventurous and exciting tone, while The Treasure in the Forest gradually becomes darker and more mysterious. In Treasure Island, the treasure hunt leads to conflicts with pirates and other dangers, whereas in The Treasure in the Forest, the hidden treasure itself is protected by poisonous thorns.

Thus, both stories use hidden treasure, maps, remote islands and dangerous journeys as important elements, but Treasure Island emphasizes adventure and conflict, while The Treasure in the Forest emphasizes mystery, suspense and the destructive consequences of greed.
