Neighbours

Tim Winton • Short Story 1

Summary of “Neighbours”

“Neighbours” by Tim Winton is a story about a newly married young couple who move into a multicultural and multilingual neighbourhood. It shows that language, culture and social differences cannot prevent people from developing love, kindness and human relationships.

When the young couple first moved into the neighbourhood, they felt uncomfortable and suspicious because most of their neighbours were European migrants. A Macedonian family lived on one side and a Polish widower on the other. Their small house looked like an elegant cottage, but the unfamiliar sounds of spitting, washing and watering the streets made them feel like sojourners in a foreign land. The Macedonian family shouted so loudly that the couple initially thought they were fighting. They also found the neighbours' customs strange and difficult to understand.

Gradually, however, their relationship with the neighbours improved. When the couple started a kitchen garden, the neighbours gave them advice about spacing, hilling and mulching, and the Macedonian woman gave them garlic cloves. When their henhouse collapsed, the Polish widower rebuilt it for them even though they could not understand his language. The couple also began exchanging vegetables, firewood and other gifts with their neighbours. These actions showed that kindness and cooperation can overcome linguistic and cultural barriers.

The couple's neighbours were especially caring when the young woman became pregnant. The whole neighbourhood showed happiness and concern. People gave them gifts, suggested names for the baby, predicted that it would be a boy, knitted clothes for it and continued helping the couple. Although the young woman sometimes felt flattered, grateful, irritated and uncomfortable by the attention, the neighbours clearly showed their positive attitude, love and concern.

When the woman went into labour, the neighbours gathered around their fences and waited anxiously. After the baby boy was born, they cheered from outside. Seeing their genuine happiness and support, the young man began to weep. His tears showed how deeply touched he was and also expressed his regret for having misunderstood and judged the neighbours when he first arrived. He finally understood that neighbours are valuable companions who help and support one another in both difficult and joyful moments.

The phrase “fractured dialogue” refers to the broken or incomplete communication between people who speak different languages. However, the neighbours communicate through actions, gestures and kindness even when words fail.

The young man's study of the twentieth-century novel had not prepared him for the genuine warmth and compassion he experienced in real life. He realized that real human relationships can be much more powerful than ideas found in books. The story therefore emphasizes multiculturalism, tolerance, cooperation, compassion and the universal bond of humanity. It also supports the idea that neighbours can become like family when they share, care and help one another.

Understanding the Text

Answer the following questions:

a. Describe how the young couple’s house looked like.

The young couple’s house was small, but its high ceilings and paned windows gave it the appearance of an elegant cottage. From his study window, the young man could see the rooftops, used car yards, and the Moreton Bay figs in the park where they walked their dog.

b. How did the young couple identify their neighbours in the beginning of their arrival?

The young couple identified their neighbours in the beginning by the sounds of spitting, washing, and watering the streets at daybreak. These unfamiliar sounds made them feel as if they were living in a foreign land.

c. How did the neighbours help the young couple in the kitchen garden?

The neighbours helped the young couple in their kitchen garden by giving them advice about spacing, hilling, and mulching. The Macedonian woman also gave the young woman a bagful of garlic cloves to plant.

d. Why were the people in the neighbourhood surprised at the role of the young man and his wife in their family?

The people in the neighbourhood were surprised because the young woman worked at a hospital while the young man stayed at home working on his thesis. He also cooked dinner for his wife when she returned home from work. This was different from the traditional family roles they were familiar with.

e. How did the neighbours respond to the woman’s pregnancy?

The neighbours responded to the woman’s pregnancy with great interest, care, and happiness. They smiled at the couple and gave them gifts. The man in the deli gave the woman chocolates and the young man packets of cigarettes. Italian women suggested names for the baby, while Greek women felt the woman’s belly and predicted that she would have a boy. The woman next door knitted a baby suit with booties and a beanie, and the Polish widower continued helping them by bringing wood.

f. Why did the young man begin to weep at the end of the story?

The young man began to weep at the end of the story because he was deeply touched by the love, care, and support shown by the neighbours. He had not expected such strong human feelings from people who were culturally and linguistically different from them.

g. Why do you think the author did not characterize the persons in the story with proper names?

I think the author did not give the characters proper names because he wanted to make the story more universal rather than limit it to particular individuals. The young couple and their neighbours represent people from different cultural and linguistic backgrounds. Through them, the writer shows that humanity, love, and compassion can exist among people even when they have different languages, cultures, and social customs.

Reference to the Context

a. The story shows that linguistic and cultural barriers do not create any obstacle in human relationship. Cite some examples from the story where the neighbours have transcended such barriers.

The story shows that linguistic and cultural barriers do not create any obstacle in human relationships. Humanity and love are such feelings that connect people. They do not look at race, caste, language, nationality, or culture. The love, respect, and kindness of a person towards others show their true humanity. In this story also, there are some misunderstandings between the young couple and their neighbours because of their different languages and cultures. However, when the young couple needed help, their neighbours helped them a lot. Some examples where the neighbours transcended such barriers are as follows:

  • - In spite of their different languages and cultures, the neighbours gave advice to the young couple about spacing, hilling, and mulching the vegetables in their kitchen garden.
  • - The Polish widower rebuilt the falling henhouse of the young couple although they could not understand his words.
  • - The young couple offered heads of cabbage and received gifts of grapes and firewood from their neighbours.
  • - The neighbours gave them chocolates and other gifts, and the woman next door knitted a suit for their baby when the couple were going to become parents.

b. The last sentence of the story reads “The twentieth-century novel had not prepared him for this.” In your view, what differences did the young man find between twentieth-century novels and human relations?

In my view, the young man had been studying twentieth-century novels and had developed certain ideas about people and human relationships. At first, his experience in the new community seemed to support his negative impression. The people in the neighbourhood appeared noisy and annoying to him. They used to make loud noises and shout at each other, and even the small children seemed to have poor sanitation. However, when he and his wife needed help, all the neighbours helped them without expecting anything in return. Their way of helping each other in need, sharing things, and sharing happiness deeply touched his heart. He realized that real human relationships were much warmer and more compassionate than what he had expected from his study of twentieth-century novels. Thus, he found a huge difference between his ideas from literature and the reality of human relations, and realized that the novel he was researching had not prepared him for such genuine human feelings.

c. A Nepali proverb says, “Neighbors are companions for wedding procession as well as for funeral procession.” Does this proverb apply in the story? Justify.

Yes, this proverb applies to the story. Neighbours are the real companions of life who share our lives in various situations. They are needed at every step of life, whether in joy or sorrow. They are companions for both wedding processions and funeral processions. Here in the story, we find the involvement of the neighbours in various events. The story mainly focuses on three close neighbours who are seen living their lives by sharing and caring for each other. We find the concept of sharing, caring, and helping among the neighbours throughout the story. During the pregnancy, the young woman was cared for, assured, and presented with gifts by the people of her neighbourhood. The neighbours are also seen teaching each other, enjoying together, and helping one another in different situations. Thus, the neighbours in the story are seen as the best companions, as mentioned in the proverb.

d. The author has dealt with an issue of multiculturalism in the story. Why do you think multiculturalism has become a major issue in the present world?

Multiculturalism is the way in which a society deals with cultural diversity, both at the national and community levels. Multiculturalism can take place on a nationwide scale or within a nation's communities. It may occur naturally through immigration or when people from different cultures come together in the same society. I think multiculturalism has become a major issue in the present world because, along with bringing people of different ethnic and cultural backgrounds together, it can also create various social and adaptation challenges. Migration is another major reason for multiculturalism. People travel across different places and countries and settle in places where they find proper facilities and jobs. As a result, people from different cultures and backgrounds are often found living in the same city or region. Due to differences in language, culture, and social customs, many people in neighbourhoods may not know or understand each other properly. This can sometimes create misunderstanding and distance among them. Therefore, multiculturalism has become a major issue in the present world because people need greater understanding, tolerance, cooperation, and respect to live peacefully with people from different cultural backgrounds.

Reference Beyond the Text

a. Write an essay on Celebration of Childbirth in my Community.

CHILDBIRTH IN MY COMMUNITY

Childbirth celebration is a ceremony or ritual to celebrate the birth of a child with joy and happiness. My community is a Hindu-majority community with people from different castes such as Brahmins, Chhetris, Newars, Magars, and others. Due to the majority of Hindus, people in my community consider a child as a gift from God. Especially girls are considered goddesses and are associated with Laxmi and Saraswati. Many good works are started by worshipping girls.

When a baby is born in my community, people congratulate the parents and celebrate the birth with joy. When the child becomes six days old, there is a ritual known as Chhaiti. On this day, it is believed that God comes to write the future of the baby. Therefore, a lamp is kept burning throughout the night, and a paper and pen are placed below the child's pillow so that God can come and write the child's future.

After that, on the eleventh day of childbirth, there is a naming ceremony called Nwaran. On this day, the child is given a name. When the child is about five or six months old, a weaning ceremony is held in which the child is fed different kinds of food, especially rice. This day is also known as the rice-feeding ceremony.

This is how people in my community celebrate the birth of a child with joy, rituals, and blessings.

b. Do the people in your community respond with similar reactions upon pregnancy and childbirth as depicted in the story? Give a couple of examples.

Yes, people in my community also respond with similar reactions to pregnancy and childbirth as depicted in the story. Like the community in the story, our culture also gives importance to pregnancy and childbirth. In our community, a pregnant woman is blessed by her relatives and is given a lot of advice and care. Pregnant women are helped in different aspects, especially regarding food and nutrition.

After learning about a woman's pregnancy, community members, especially women, visit her at her home and spend time discussing her condition and well-being. They express their happiness by giving gifts such as chocolates, clothes, and other useful items. They also suggest many names for the unborn child, both male and female.

In this way, people in my community express their love, care, and affection towards pregnant women and celebrate the arrival of a new child in the family.
