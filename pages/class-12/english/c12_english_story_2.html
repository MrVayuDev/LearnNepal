A Respectable Woman

Kate Chopin • Short Story 2

Summary of “A Respectable Woman”

“A Respectable Woman” is a short story by the American writer Kate Chopin. The story explores love, attraction, marriage, morality and the conflict between personal feelings and social expectations. It focuses on Mrs. Baroda, a respectable married woman, and her unexpected attraction towards Gouvernail, her husband’s friend.

Mrs. Baroda is disappointed when her husband, Gaston, tells her that his friend Gouvernail will stay with them at their plantation for a week or two. She had expected to spend some peaceful time alone with her husband. Before meeting Gouvernail, she imagines him as a tall, slim and cynical man who wears glasses, but he turns out to be quite different. He is slim, quiet, gentle and reserved. Unlike the frank and talkative Gaston, Gouvernail prefers peace and solitude.

At first, Mrs. Baroda tries to make Gouvernail more sociable by offering him friendly and wordy hospitality, but she becomes increasingly confused by her own feelings towards him. Although she finds him attractive and develops a strong desire to touch him, she controls herself because she considers herself a respectable woman. She does not reveal these feelings to Gaston because she believes that some emotional struggles must be faced privately and that expressing them could harm her marriage and her husband’s friendship with Gouvernail.

Eventually, Gouvernail leaves, and Mrs. Baroda feels relieved. Later, however, she tells Gaston that she has “overcome everything” and suggests that Gouvernail should visit them again. This unexpected ending creates a twist. It can be understood either as Mrs. Baroda overcoming her attraction and becoming comfortable with Gouvernail, or as her becoming willing to accept her feelings. The story leaves her final attitude deliberately uncertain, showing the complex conflict between desire, morality and social respectability.

Understanding the Text

Answer the following questions:

a. Why was Mrs. Baroda unhappy with the information about Gouvernail’s visit to their farm?

Mrs. Baroda was unhappy about Gouvernail’s visit because she had been looking forward to spending some peaceful and uninterrupted time alone with her husband after his arrival at the farm.

b. How was Gouvernail different from Mrs. Baroda’s expectation?

Baroda had imagined Gouvernail as a tall, slim and cynical man who wore glasses and kept his hands in his pockets. However, the real Gouvernail was quite different. Although he was slim, he was neither very tall nor cynical, and he did not wear glasses or keep his hands in his pockets.

c. How does Mrs. Baroda compare Gouvernail with her husband?

Mrs. Baroda compares the two men mainly on the basis of their personalities. Her husband, Gaston Baroda, is frank, friendly and talkative, whereas Gouvernail is quiet, reserved and less open in conversation.

d. Why and how did Mrs. Baroda try to change Gouvernail’s solitary habits?

Mrs. Baroda tried to change Gouvernail’s solitary habits because she wanted him to feel comfortable and at home at their farm. Being a frank and sociable woman, she attempted to make him more talkative and involved by offering him warm and wordy hospitality.

e. How does Gaston disagree with his wife on Gouvernail’s character?

Gaston disagrees with his wife’s opinion of Gouvernail. While Mrs. Baroda considers his quiet and solitary nature somewhat troublesome, Gaston believes that Gouvernail causes no trouble and simply prefers a peaceful and quiet environment without unnecessary commotion.

f. Why is Gaston surprised with his wife’s expression towards the end of the story?

Gaston is surprised because Mrs. Baroda had initially disliked the idea of Gouvernail staying with them and had wanted him to leave. However, towards the end, she unexpectedly suggests, on her own, that Gouvernail should visit them again. Her sudden change of attitude surprises Gaston.

Reference to the Context

a. What is the cause of conflict in Mrs. Baroda’s mind? What role does Mrs. Baroda ‘being a respectable woman’ play in the story?

The main cause of conflict in Mrs. Baroda’s mind is her growing attraction towards Gouvernail and her sense of social and moral responsibility. She feels strongly attracted to him and even wishes to touch his face and lips with her sensitive fingers. However, she controls these feelings because she considers herself a respectable woman. Her sense of respectability prevents her from acting on her attraction and makes her struggle between her emotions and her moral values.

b. Sketch the character of Gouvernail and contrast it with Gaston.

Gouvernail is Mr. Baroda’s college friend who comes to stay with the Barodas for a week or two at their sugar plantation. He is slim, attractive, lovable and inoffensive. Unlike Gaston, he is quiet, reserved and not very talkative. He enjoys sitting on the portico, smoking a cigar and appreciating the peaceful surroundings. He is also undemanding and is satisfied with whatever the Barodas provide for him.

In contrast, Gaston Baroda is frank, friendly and talkative. He is more sociable and outgoing, whereas Gouvernail is reserved and prefers solitude. Their personalities are therefore quite different. Gouvernail can be seen as an introverted and peaceful person, while Gaston is more extroverted and sociable.

c. Why does Mrs. Baroda not disclose her feelings towards Gouvernail to her husband?

Mrs. Baroda does not reveal her feelings towards Gouvernail because she considers them a personal struggle that she must control herself. As a respectable woman, she is unwilling to admit that she has developed an attraction towards another man. She also fears that revealing her feelings could create problems in her marriage and might affect the friendship between Gaston and Gouvernail. Therefore, she chooses to keep her feelings to herself and fight the emotional conflict alone.

d. The last three sentences of the story bring a kind of twist. After reading these three sentences, how do you analyze Mrs. Baroda’s attitude towards Gouvernail?

The last three sentences create an unexpected twist in Mrs. Baroda’s attitude towards Gouvernail. At first, she disliked his visit and struggled with her attraction towards him. By the end, however, she tells her husband that she has “overcome everything” and willingly suggests that Gouvernail should visit them again.

This ending can be understood in two ways. First, Mrs. Baroda may have overcome her attraction towards Gouvernail and decided that she can now behave normally and politely with him. On the other hand, it can also suggest that she has overcome her hesitation and is now more willing to accept her feelings. The ending is deliberately ambiguous, leaving the reader to decide whether she has conquered her emotions or simply become more comfortable with them.

Reference Beyond the Text

a. The entry of an outsider into a family has been a recurring subject in both literature and films. Narrate a story, real or imaginative, where an outsider’s arrival destroys the intimate relationship between the husband and wife and causes a breakup in the marital relationship without direct fault of anyone.

ABOUT LOVE

Anton Chekhov’s “About Love” is a story that deals with love, sacrifice and the difficulties created by social and moral restrictions. Its third story shares some similarities with Kate Chopin’s “A Respectable Woman”, especially in the way an outsider influences the relationship between a married couple.

The story presents the love between Alyohin and Anna, the wife of Dimitry Luganovich. Alyohin meets Dimitry Luganovich, a judge, after being elected as an honorary justice of the peace. Later, he visits Luganovich’s house for dinner, where he meets Anna. He is immediately attracted to her beauty, youth and intelligence.

Alyohin and Anna gradually become close. They spend time together, exchange gifts, go to the theatre and share their joys and sorrows. Alyohin falls deeply in love with Anna, but his moral beliefs and social responsibilities prevent him from expressing his feelings openly. As a result, both of them suffer emotionally. Their feelings remain unspoken because Anna is already married and both are concerned about social and moral consequences.

Eventually, the Luganovich family decides to leave the place. Alyohin goes to the railway station to say goodbye to Anna. During their final meeting, they realize how deeply they love each other. They cry, embrace and Alyohin finally expresses his love. However, they must separate because Anna is leaving for Crimea for medical treatment. Their relationship ends not because either of them intentionally wanted to hurt another person, but because of circumstances, marriage and social morality.

b. Suppose you are a mature girl/boy and your family members are giving you pressure for getting married. Write in about 200 words describing what qualities you would like to get in your future husband/wife.

Marriage is a sacred relationship between two people. It is a lifelong commitment that creates family ties and involves social, emotional and legal responsibilities. Therefore, choosing a life partner is an important decision that should be made carefully rather than under pressure. Before marriage, a person should understand the nature, values and qualities of the person with whom they are going to spend their life.

If my family members put pressure on me to get married, I would look for the following qualities in my future wife:

  • Loving and Caring: She should be loving and caring towards me as well as my family members. She should treat everyone with affection, respect and understanding.
  • Well-Mannered: She should be polite, humble and respectful towards others. Good manners and positive behaviour are important for maintaining healthy relationships.
  • Beautiful in Character: She should have a good heart and a pleasant personality. More than physical appearance, I would value kindness, honesty and good character.
  • Responsible and Respectable: She should be responsible towards herself, her partner and the family. She should understand her duties and carry them out with sincerity and dignity.
  • Educated: She should be well-educated and understand the importance of education. She should also encourage others to learn and improve themselves.

In my view, these qualities would help create a strong, respectful and happy marital relationship.
