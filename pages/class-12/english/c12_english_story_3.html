A Devoted Son

Anita Desai • Short Story 3

Summary of “A Devoted Son”

“A Devoted Son” is a realistic short story written by Anita Desai, a well-known Indian English writer. The story is set in a middle class Indian family and mainly deals with the relationship between parents and their children. It shows how parents have great ambitions for their children and how a son tries to fulfil his duties towards his parents. The story also highlights the problems of old age, generation gap, parental sacrifice, and the difference between physical care and emotional happiness.

The story revolves around Rakesh and his father, Mr. Varma. Rakesh is born to illiterate parents who make many sacrifices to provide him with a good education. His father works at a kerosene dealer’s depot, while his mother looks after the household. Rakesh studies hard and becomes a brilliant student. When he tops the medical examination, the whole family and neighbourhood celebrate his success. His achievement becomes a great source of pride for his parents, especially because he is the first son in the family to receive such a high level of education.

After completing his medical studies, Rakesh goes to the USA on a scholarship. He works at a prestigious hospital and earns the respect of his American colleagues. However, instead of staying abroad, he returns to his own country and starts working at a city hospital. Through his hard work, he rises to an important position and later becomes the director of the hospital. He eventually opens his own clinic and becomes one of the most successful and wealthy doctors in the town.

Rakesh also respects his parents’ wishes regarding his marriage. He marries a simple and traditional village girl chosen by his mother. She adjusts well to the family and does not try to separate Rakesh from his parents.

After Rakesh’s mother dies, his father becomes weak and starts suffering from various health problems. Rakesh takes great care of him because he is both a devoted son and a doctor. He brings his father tea, reads the newspaper to him, helps him rest comfortably, and takes care of his food and medicines. However, Rakesh becomes extremely strict about his father’s diet. He does not allow him to eat sweets, fried food, and other things he enjoys. As a result, Mr. Varma becomes unhappy and frustrated. He even tries to secretly bribe his grandchildren with money to buy him jalebis.

Thus, the story presents an interesting contrast between Rakesh’s devotion and his father’s desire for freedom and happiness. Rakesh genuinely loves his father and wants to keep him healthy, but his excessive care and strictness make his father feel miserable. Anita Desai therefore shows that elderly people need not only food, medicine, and physical care but also love, companionship, respect, independence, and emotional support.

The story ultimately teaches us that true devotion means understanding and respecting the feelings and wishes of our loved ones, rather than simply making decisions for them. It also reminds us that parents sacrifice greatly for their children, and children should care for their parents with love, patience, and understanding.

Key Moral: Elderly people need not only food, medicine, and physical care but also love, companionship, respect, independence, and emotional support.

Understanding the Text

Answer the following questions:

a. How did the morning papers bring an ambience of celebration to the Varma family?

The morning papers brought an ambience of celebration to the Varma family because they announced that Rakesh had topped the medical examination. His remarkable achievement became a great matter of pride and joy for the entire family.

b. How did the community celebrate Rakesh’s success?

The community celebrated Rakesh’s success by visiting his little yellow house at the end of the road. They congratulated his parents on their son’s remarkable achievement and praised Rakesh as well. The house and garden soon became filled with the sights and sounds of a festival, including garlands, party clothes, and gifts.

c. Why was Rakesh’s success a special matter of discussion in the neighbourhood?

Rakesh’s success was a special matter of discussion in the neighbourhood because he was the first son in his family to receive an education. His parents had made many sacrifices to send him to school and later to medical college. Therefore, his achievement was a matter of pride for both his family and the neighbours. Moreover, Rakesh’s respectful behavior of touching his father’s feet immediately after seeing his results made his success even more remarkable.

d. How does the author make fun with the words ‘America’ and ‘the USA’?

The author makes fun of the words ‘America’ and ‘the USA’ by showing how Rakesh’s father proudly learned to call America ‘the USA’. He even taught the whole family to use ‘the USA’ instead of ‘America’, which he considered a term used by their ignorant neighbours. Through this, the author humorously shows the father’s pride and his desire to sound sophisticated.

e. How does the author characterize Rakesh’s wife?

The author characterizes Rakesh’s wife as a simple, uneducated, and traditional village girl. She was chosen by Rakesh’s mother and was the daughter of her childhood friend. She was calm, good-natured, and easily adjusted to the family. She was also beautiful and did not try to persuade Rakesh to leave his parents and live separately.

f. Describe how Rakesh rises in his career.

Rakesh gradually achieved great success in his medical career. After completing his M.D., he won a scholarship and went to the USA to continue his career at a prestigious hospital. He received praise from his American colleagues and later returned home. He worked at a city hospital, quickly rose to a high administrative position, and became its director. Later, he established his own clinic and became known as one of the best and richest doctors in the town.

g. How does the author describe Rakesh’s family background?

The author describes Rakesh as a son born to illiterate parents. His father worked for forty years at a kerosene dealer’s depot, while his mother spent her life working in the kitchen. Despite their limited education and simple background, his parents made great sacrifices to provide him with a good education. Their sacrifices helped Rakesh achieve success and become an excellent doctor.

h. What is the impact of Rakesh’s mother’s death on his father?

Rakesh’s mother’s death had a negative impact on his father. After losing his wife, the old man quickly became weak and frequently fell ill. He developed various physical complaints and spent most of his time lying on his string bed. He also became increasingly dependent on his son and required constant care and attention.

i. What did Rakesh do to make his father’s old age more comfortable?

Rakesh took great care of his father in his old age. He brought him morning tea in his favorite brass tumbler and read the morning newspaper to him. In the evening, he encouraged his father to spend time in the garden and arranged pillows and bolsters for him. During summer nights, he also had his father’s bed moved onto the lawn and helped him settle down comfortably. He also carefully supervised his father’s diet and medicines.

j. Why did the old man try to bribe his grandchildren?

The old man tried to bribe his grandchildren because he was not allowed to eat sweets and other food he enjoyed. Rakesh had strictly restricted his diet, so Varma secretly offered his grandchildren money to buy him jalebis from the shop. He promised them some of the remaining money if they would bring him the sweets without telling their parents.

k. Are Mr. Varma’s complaints about his diet reasonable? How?

Yes, Mr. Varma’s complaints about his diet are reasonable to some extent. Although Rakesh restricted his father’s diet because he was concerned about his health, the old man was deprived of almost all the foods he enjoyed. He was given mainly dry bread, boiled lentils, and boiled vegetables, while fried and sweet foods were completely avoided. Therefore, Mr. Varma felt unhappy and frustrated because his food had become too strict and tasteless. However, Rakesh’s intention was to protect his father from further illness.

Reference to the Context

a. How did the Varma couple make sacrifices for their son’s higher education?

Mr. Varma worked at a kerosene dealer’s depot, while Mrs. Varma was a housewife who looked after the household. Despite their simple background, they made many sacrifices for their son’s higher education. Mr. Varma himself had never received a formal education, but he wanted his son to have the opportunities he had missed. Rakesh was the first son in the family to receive an education. Therefore, the Varma couple worked hard and sacrificed their time, money, and many comforts to send Rakesh to school and later to medical college for a better future.

b. Mr. Varma suffers from diseases one after another after his wife’s death. Would he have enjoyed better health if she had not died before him? Give reasons.

Yes, I think Mr. Varma might have enjoyed better health if his wife had not died before him. He began to decline soon after her death. Before losing his wife, he had someone who had shared his life for many years and provided him with companionship and care. After her death, he became mentally and physically weaker and started suffering from various illnesses. Losing a person with whom one has spent decades can cause deep emotional pain and loneliness. Therefore, if his wife had remained alive, her companionship and care might have helped him maintain better physical and mental health.

c. Dr. Rakesh is divided between a doctor and a son. As a son, he loves his father and worries about his weakening health but as a doctor, he is strict on his father’s diet and medicine. In your view, what else could Rakesh have done to make his father’s final years more comfortable?

There is no doubt that Rakesh was a devoted son and an excellent doctor. However, he was divided between his responsibilities as a doctor and as a son. In my view, Rakesh could have been more gentle, polite, and respectful towards his father during his final years. Instead of making every decision for him, he could have given his elderly father more independence and allowed him to express his own wishes. He could also have given him more personal space and listened patiently to his feelings and concerns. Rakesh’s strict behavior made his father feel uncomfortable and miserable. Therefore, by speaking to him more kindly and respecting his wishes, Rakesh could have made his father’s final years more peaceful and comfortable.

d. What does the story say about the relationship between grandfather and grandchildren?

Desai’s story “A Devoted Son” presents a loving and close relationship between the grandfather and his grandchildren. There is a strong bond of affection between them. Because of their close relationship, the grandfather once tried to bribe his grandchildren to bring him jalebis. He took advantage of their innocence and childish nature to satisfy his own desire for sweets. Although he loved his grandchildren very much, his action also showed his selfishness and encouraged them to do something secretly. Thus, the relationship between the grandfather and his grandchildren is shown as affectionate, innocent, close, and somewhat tricky.

e. Do you call Rakesh a devoted son? Give reasons.

Yes, I consider Rakesh a devoted son. He always tried to fulfil his parents’ wishes and followed their advice. He worked hard in his studies, accepted his parents’ decision regarding his marriage, and returned to his own country after completing his education in the USA, even though he could have earned more by staying there. He also continued to take care of his father despite being very busy with his professional life. Although he was sometimes too strict with his father and failed to understand his emotional needs, he genuinely wanted his father to remain healthy and comfortable. Therefore, Rakesh can be called a devoted son because he often put aside his own wishes and dedicated himself to the well-being of his parents.

Reference Beyond the Text

a. Write an essay on “The Parents’ Ambition for their Children in Nepali Society.” You must give at least five examples.

The Parents’ Ambition for their Children in Nepali Society

Parents have great hopes and ambitions for their children in Nepali society. Most parents want their children to become successful, respected, and financially secure in life. They often wish to see their children become doctors, engineers, teachers, pilots, civil servants, or successful businesspeople. Their ambitions usually come from their desire to provide their children with a better and more comfortable future.

For example, some parents want their children to become doctors because they believe it is a respected and successful profession. Similarly, many parents encourage their children to become engineers because they consider engineering to provide good career opportunities. Some parents want their children to become teachers because teaching is regarded as a respectable profession that contributes to society. Others dream of their children becoming civil servants because government jobs are considered secure and prestigious. Likewise, some parents encourage their children to become pilots or successful businesspeople because they believe these careers can bring financial stability and social recognition.

Parents’ ambitions can motivate children to work hard and achieve their goals. However, sometimes parents impose their own dreams on their children without considering the children’s interests, abilities, and choices. As a result, some children are forced to follow a career path they do not enjoy. This can create stress, disappointment, and frustration in their lives.

Therefore, parents should guide and support their children rather than forcing their own ambitions upon them. They should listen to their children’s interests and respect their choices. A child is more likely to succeed and remain happy when parents provide encouragement while allowing them to choose a suitable path for their future.

b. “Medicines replace our diets in old age.” What can be done to make old people less dependent on medicine?

As people grow older, their bodies gradually become weaker and less efficient than they were in their younger years. Their immune system may also become weaker, making them more vulnerable to different illnesses. As a result, older people may need medicines more frequently. However, a healthy lifestyle can help them remain active and reduce the risk of some health problems.

To make older people less dependent on medicine, they can follow these healthy habits:

  • They should exercise regularly through activities such as walking, jogging, dancing, or other suitable physical activities.
  • They should eat balanced and nutritious meals containing different food groups, including carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals.
  • They should get sufficient sleep and maintain a regular sleeping routine.
  • They should drink enough water to stay properly hydrated, especially during hot weather.
  • They should avoid both excessive sleep and insufficient sleep, as either can affect their health and energy.
  • They should stay away from harmful habits such as smoking, alcohol, and drug use.

However, medicines prescribed by a doctor should not be stopped or replaced without medical advice. A healthy lifestyle can support better health, but proper treatment is still important when it is needed.

c. Write an essay on “Care of Elderly Citizens” in about 300 words.

Care of Elderly Citizens

Elderly people are those who have reached the later stage of human life. Old age is a natural part of life, and during this period people need love, affection, respect, and proper care. Caring for elderly citizens is not only a social responsibility but also a moral duty of every human being. As people grow older, they may experience different physical and emotional difficulties, so they require greater attention and support from their families and society.

Taking care of elderly people does not always require expensive facilities or complicated arrangements. Most elderly people mainly need affection, companionship, respect, and a peaceful family environment. We can care for them by providing nutritious food, helping them with their daily activities, taking them for regular health check-ups, and giving them emotional support. Spending time with them and listening to their experiences can also make them feel valued and loved.

Unfortunately, in today’s busy lifestyle, some people consider elderly parents a burden. They may not give enough time or attention to their parents and sometimes prefer to send them to old-age homes instead of caring for them at home. Although old-age homes can provide necessary facilities in some situations, abandoning elderly parents simply because they are old is not a responsible approach.

Every society should respect and protect its elderly citizens. Governments can make laws and provide facilities for their welfare, but laws alone cannot guarantee their happiness. We must also change our attitudes towards old age and understand the needs of elderly people.

Therefore, caring for elderly citizens should be regarded as an important responsibility of families and society. By giving them love, respect, care, and a peaceful environment, we can help them live their final years with dignity and happiness.
